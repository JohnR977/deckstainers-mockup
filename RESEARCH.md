## What every top site has
1. H1 with the city name, a Free Estimate button and a click-to-call phone number in the header (sticky).
2. Trust badges near the top: licensed and insured, years in business, star rating.
3. A service overview: cleaning, stripping, sanding, staining, sealing, repairs.
4. A numbered 3–5 step process.
5. Finish options: transparent, semi-transparent, solid, hardwood oil.
6. Google review excerpts.
7. Before/after gallery.
8. FAQ with 5–8 questions.
9. Service-area section with city links and a map.
10. A closing CTA and a footer with name, address and phone.

## Gaps a new site can own in Chattanooga
- Nobody publishes deck staining prices. A per-sq-ft estimate calculator would be unique locally.
- No deck stain visualizer anywhere. That 1 Painter's is for paint only.
- Warranty terms are vague on most sites. Spell them out.
- Photo-upload estimates are rare. They suit a 1–2 man company that wants to skip drive-out visits.
- Maintenance plans get mentioned but never priced. Recurring plans also feed the CRM.
- Local sites name no brands and cite no experts.
- Local deck content is thin: Signal Mountain shade and mildew, the humid valley, log homes, best season.

## What homeowners ask, with sourced answers
- **Cost.** Angi 2026: average $850, typical $550–$1,250, about $2–$4/sq ft (https://www.angi.com/articles/how-much-does-it-cost-stain-deck.htm). Bob Vila: average $775 (https://www.bobvila.com/articles/cost-to-stain-a-deck/).
- **How often to restain.** Angi: every 2–3 yrs. Bob Vila: oil 3–5, water 2–3, solid 4–5. USDA Handbook 647: semitransparent lasts 3–6 yrs on rough weathered decking, 2–3 on new smooth decking (https://www.fpl.fs.usda.gov/documnts/usda/ah647.pdf).
- **Oil vs water-based.** Fine Homebuilding (https://www.finehomebuilding.com/project-guides/decks/whats-the-difference-deck-stain).
- **Semi-transparent vs solid.** USDA FPL says solids and paint tend to peel on decks. Consumer Reports via KOMO says solid scored best in their tests (https://komonews.com/news/consumer/top-rated-deck-stains-from-consumer-reports). The disagreement makes a good honest FAQ.
- **Cleaning.** USDA FPL warns that aggressive power washing damages soft wood. Let the deck dry 2+ days before finishing (https://buildingadvisor.com/wp-content/uploads/2015/04/USDA-Deck-Finishing.pdf).
- **New pressure-treated wood.** Home Depot: 3–6 months to dry. Water-drop test.
- **Licensing.** A TN Home Improvement license is required in Hamilton County for jobs from $3,000 to $25,000 (https://tn.gov/commerce/regboards/hi/home-improvement-licensing-requirements.html).

## Visualizer findings
- Duradek Dek-Vision, Chameleon Power, Renoworks and FacadeColorizer exist, but they are sold to manufacturers or locked to one brand. No deck contractor in the review had one.
- **Recommended build:** have the homeowner trace or click the deck area, then tint the wood with a luminance-preserving color blend in the browser (opacity low for semi-transparent, high for solid). Colors stay accurate and the rest of the photo is untouched. Meta's Segment Anything (Apache 2.0) can auto-mask later.
- Pure AI image editing (for example OpenAI's image edit) regenerates the whole picture and can change the house. Keep it optional, as an "enhance" pass at most.
- Gate the full-size render behind name and email so every render becomes a CRM lead.
