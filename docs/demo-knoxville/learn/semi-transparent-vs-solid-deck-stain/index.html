Semi-transparent vs. solid stain on a deck

This is the one question where two trusted sources give opposite advice. We'll show you both and tell you how we decide on the job.

Side 1: the wood scientists

The U.S. Forest Products Laboratory has studied wood finishes for decades. Their advice for decks is clear: use penetrating finishes, not film-forming ones. They write that paints and solid-color stains "are prone to trap moisture and fail by peeling"1. Another of their guides says paint and solid-color stains "are not appropriate for fully exposed decks"2, and a third says opaque stains shouldn't be used on decks and fences3.

Their own table gives semi-transparent stain on decking about 2–3 years on new smooth wood and 3–6 years on weathered, rougher wood2.

Side 2: Consumer Reports' testing

Consumer Reports tests deck stains on panels outdoors. Their tester told KOMO News that "solid color staining always fares best in our testing," and that a solid color can last seven to 10 years, while clearer stains may need redoing every one to three years4.

Why they disagree

They're measuring different things. Consumer Reports rates how the finish looks and protects over time on test panels. The Forest Products Lab focuses on how the finish fails on a real deck, where water gets in at the board ends, cracks and nail holes. When a solid stain does fail on a floor, it peels, and peeling means stripping. When a semi-transparent wears, it fades, and fading means a quick recoat.

How we decide

We lean semi-transparent when…

  • It's a walking surface: deck floors, stairs
  • The deck is shady or damp (think the wooded lots toward the Smokies)
  • You want to see the wood grain
  • You want cheap, easy maintenance coats

Solid can make sense when…

  • It's vertical: railings, skirting, fences, siding
  • The wood is mismatched or patched and you want one even color
  • You understand a failed solid coat means stripping later
  • There's good airflow and drainage

A popular middle path: semi-transparent on the floor, solid on the railings. You get a grain look underfoot and a crisp, long-lasting color on the parts you look at most.

Our written warranty reflects this. Solid stain on deck floors gets a shorter term, and we tell you why up front.
